Kanaka Durga Temple, Vijayawada

India · Indrakeeladri, Vijayawada, Andhra Pradesh

Kanaka Durga on Indrakeeladri Hill — Vijayawada’s principal Devi temple and a flagship Andhra Pradesh pilgrimage stop.

देवी जी · Devi / Shakti

Temple Glimpse

Mythology & Significance

दुर्गां देवीं शरणमहं प्रपद्ये

I take refuge in Goddess Durga

Devi Mahatmya refuge-verse; fitting Indrakeeladri’s Kanaka Durga

Significance in mythology

Kanaka Durga on Indrakeeladri overlooks the Krishna. Shakta tradition links the hill with Durga’s victory over mahishasura-class forces in the Devi Mahatmya cycle and with regional Andhra mahatmya that place the goddess as guardian of the river crossing.

Vijayawada (‘city of victory’) is popularly read through this theology: the golden (kanaka) mother on the hill confers victory, protection, and prosperity to the Krishna basin.

Local stories & beliefs

Dasara celebrations transform the hill into a city-wide festival of the goddess. River–hill geography is read as Durga’s own seat of victory and protection.

Local Telugu devotion calls her the golden mother; families climb for Varalakshmi, Dasara, and ordinary Fridays believing Indrakeeladri answers household vows.

Scriptural & traditional links: Devi Mahatmya / Durga tradition, Andhra Shakta sthala purana, Regional Krishna-river tirtha lore, Accounts summarised from widely cited Puranic, epic, and pilgrimage traditions; local tellings may differ.

Known for: Hilltop Shakti shrine on the Krishna
