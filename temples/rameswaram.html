Ramanathaswamy Temple

India · Rameswaram Island, Tamil Nadu

A Jyotirlinga and Char Dham seat linked to Rama’s worship of Shiva before crossing to Lanka — famous for its corridors and 22 sacred wells.

Mythology & Significance

श्री रामेश्वराय नमः

Salutation to Rameshwara — Shiva worshipped by Rama

Ramayana-linked consecration of the lingam on this shore

Significance in mythology

Rameswaram’s Ramanathaswamy temple is both a Jyotirlinga and a seat of Adi Shankaracharya’s Char Dham. In the Ramayana tradition, Rama worshipped Shiva here before (and in some tellings after) the battle with Ravana, installing a lingam on the seashore. The temple thus joins Shaiva Jyotirlinga theology with the Ramayana’s geography of exile, devotion, and return.

Local stories & beliefs

Pilgrims bathe in the temple’s twenty-two theerthams before darshan — a ritual sequence believed to purify body and mind. Local belief holds that the corridors and wells mark Rama’s sacred footprint on the island.

Dress customs (dhoti/veshti, saree) and theertham discipline are treated as part of respectful approach to Ramanathaswamy. The island is also woven into the wider Ramayana trail toward Dhanushkodi.

Scriptural & traditional links: Valmiki Ramayana, Shiva Purana / Jyotirlinga tradition, Skanda Purana Setu–Rameswara mahatmya lore, Accounts summarised from widely cited Puranic, epic, and pilgrimage traditions; local tellings may differ.

Known for: Longest temple corridor & 22 theerthams

Practical Darshan Details

Dress code
Traditional expectation: men often wear dhoti (veshti) without shirt for inner sanctum entry; women in saree or modest traditional dress. Rules are actively enforced — carry a dhoti/saree or hire/buy near the temple.
Darshan timings
Early morning to night with specific spa / aarti windows. Theertham bathing sequence before darshan is customary.
Special entry / passes
Special entry / quicker darshan tickets when available at official counters. Follow temple notice boards.
Lockers
Paid cloakrooms for mobiles, cameras, belts, wallets near entrances.

Restrictions at gates

Phones and leather items often not allowed in inner areas — use cloakrooms. Photography restricted in sanctum.
