Pashupatinath Temple

Nepal · Kathmandu, Nepal

Nepal’s foremost Shiva temple on the Bagmati — a UNESCO-linked sacred landscape and a living bridge between Himalayan Shaivism and Indian pilgrimage.

Mythology & Significance

ॐ पशुपतये नमः

Om, salutation to Pashupati — Lord of all beings

Classical name-mantra of Kathmandu’s great Shaiva seat

Significance in mythology

Pashupatinath in Kathmandu is Nepal’s foremost Shiva temple. Pashupati — ‘Lord of Beings’ — is an ancient form of Shiva celebrated in Himalayan and pan-Indian Shaiva devotion. The Bagmati riverside complex is a major cremation and pilgrimage landscape.

Pilgrimage literature treats Pashupatinath Temple as a tirtha — a crossing where devotion to Lord Shiva (Pashupati) is believed to bear fruit more readily than in ordinary space. Accounts summarised here follow widely cited epic, Puranic, and regional mahatmya traditions; temple priests remain the guides for ritual detail.

Local stories & beliefs

Shivaratri draws Himalayan and Indian pilgrims in vast numbers. Local Newar and Nepali traditions maintain distinctive ritual calendars around the shrine.

Proximity to Guhyeshwari Shakti Peetha creates a paired Shiva–Shakti belief for many Kathmandu valley devotees.

First-time visitors often hear several versions of the same story from pandas, taxi guides, and elderly pilgrims — a reminder that local belief is living conversation, not a single fixed text.

Scriptural & traditional links: Shaiva / Pashupata traditions, Nepalese chronicles and sthala lore, Himalayan Shaiva pilgrimage tradition, Accounts summarised from widely cited Puranic, epic, and pilgrimage traditions; local tellings may differ.

Known for: Nepal’s holiest Shiva shrine

Getting There, Stay & Food

Nearest railway
No direct Indian rail to Kathmandu; common route via Raxaul–Birgunj or flights.
Nearest airport
Tribhuvan International Airport, Kathmandu.
Local language
Nepali; Hindi widely understood; English in tourist areas.

Accommodation

Hotels across Kathmandu; guesthouses near Thamel and airport corridor.

Local food

Newari and Nepali vegetarian thalis, temple-area snacks; sattvic options common for pilgrims.

Other food options

Kathmandu offers global cuisine away from the temple core.

Practical Darshan Details

Dress code
Modest dress. Non-Hindus may face entry limits to the main sanctum — follow posted rules and guides.
Darshan timings
Early morning to evening with ritual breaks; confirm locally especially on festival days.
Special entry / passes
Security and queue systems intensify on Shivaratri; use official/local guidance only.
Lockers
Bag rules vary; travel light inside the complex.

Restrictions at gates

Photography restricted in inner areas. Respect cremation ghats — no intrusive filming.
