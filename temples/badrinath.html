Badrinath Temple

India · Chamoli district, Uttarakhand

The Himalayan Vishnu dham of the Char Dham circuit, open seasonally, with deep ties to Adi Shankaracharya’s tradition.

विष्णु जी · VishnuChar DhamChota Char Dham

Temple Glimpse

Mythology & Significance

ॐ नमो भगवते वासुदेवाय

Om, salutation to the Blessed Vasudeva

Dwadasakshari mantra of Vaishnava Badri devotion

Significance in mythology

Badrinath is the Himalayan Vishnu seat of Adi Shankaracharya’s Char Dham and also belongs to the Uttarakhand Chota Char Dham. Classical tradition associates the site with Nar-Narayan meditation and Vishnu’s presence in the Badri forest. Shankaracharya’s digvijaya narratives credit him with establishing the living pilgrimage and monastic order connected to the shrine.

Local stories & beliefs

Garhwal belief holds that the deity ‘resides’ at Badrinath in the open season and moves to Joshimath for winter worship — a seasonal theology shared with other high Himalayan dhams. Mana village, Vyas Gufa, and related spots are woven into local Mahabharata and sage lore.

Pilgrims often combine Badrinath with Kedarnath, treating the dual Vishnu–Shiva Himalayan yatra as a complete spiritual circuit.

Festivals & sacred days

Best time to go
May–June and September–October. Closed in deep winter.
Climate
High-altitude cold; sudden weather shifts common.
What to carry
Woolens, rain protection, altitude awareness meds if advised by your doctor, ID, offline maps.

Getting There, Stay & Food

Nearest railway
Rishikesh / Haridwar, then long road journey via Joshimath.
Nearest airport
Dehradun; limited helicopter options in Char Dham season.
Local language
Hindi, Garhwali; some English.

Accommodation

Temple committee and GMVN rooms, plus private hotels in Badrinath town — book ahead in peak season.

Local food

Simple vegetarian Garhwali / North Indian pilgrim food; prasadam.

Other food options

Limited; stick to sealed water and busy kitchens at altitude.

Practical Darshan Details

Dress code
Warm modest clothing. Footwear off in temple. No South-Indian-style dhoti mandate.
Darshan timings
Seasonal schedule with morning and evening aartis. Winter worship continues at Joshimath.
Special entry / passes
Online / counter systems vary by year under temple committee rules.
Lockers
Small cloak facilities near temple approaches; travel light.

Restrictions at gates

Respect plastic bans and fragile mountain ecology. Follow VIP / queue notices only from official counters.
