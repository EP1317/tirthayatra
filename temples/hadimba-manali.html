Hadimba Devi Temple, Manali

India · Manali, Kullu, Himachal Pradesh

Hadimba Devi — Manali’s iconic wooden temple in a cedar grove.

Mythology & Significance

Significance in mythology

Linked to Hadimba of the Mahabharata (Bhima’s wife in the epic’s Himalayan episode). Local Kullu–Manali identity wraps epic memory in mountain wooden architecture.

Mahabharata-linked sacred geography — battlefield, teaching, or Krishna traditions — frames the significance retained in epic and regional mahatmya.

Classical pilgrimage teaching places Hadimba Devi Temple, Manali within India’s tirtha network: a location where devotion to Goddess Hadimba is believed to ripen vows more readily than in ordinary space. Epic, Puranic, and regional mahatmya literature — not a single uniform text — sustain this sense of sacred geography.

Local stories & beliefs

In popular pilgrimage memory, Hadimba Devi Temple, Manali is especially associated with Cedar-forest pagoda temple of Manali. Guides and elders retell this identity to first-time visitors as the living ‘reason’ the tirtha draws crowds.

Local sthala-purana and priestly teaching at Hadimba Devi Temple, Manali elaborate how Goddess Hadimba blesses devotees who arrive with vows, gratitude, or grief. Festival days intensify these beliefs through processions, special alankara, and community feeding where practised.

Pilgrims commonly link practical customs — dress, queue, prasadam, and parikrama — with spiritual fruit. Such customs are living belief: they vary by region and should be followed as posted by the temple trust or endowment board.
