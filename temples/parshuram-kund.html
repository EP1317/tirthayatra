Significance in mythology

Parshuram Kund at Lohit district, Arunachal Pradesh is dedicated to Lord Parshuram / sacred kund, known especially for Makar Sankranti fair · Northeast Hindu pilgrimage search. Pilgrimage tradition places the shrine within India’s tirtha network — a seat where vows, gratitude, and family kuladevi / ishta devotion gather. Local sthala-purana and priestly teaching elaborate how Lord Parshuram / sacred kund blesses those who arrive with sincere intent; festival days intensify these beliefs through special alankara and community worship where practised.

Classical pilgrimage teaching places Parshuram Kund within India’s tirtha network: a location where devotion to Lord Parshuram / sacred kund is believed to ripen vows more readily than in ordinary space. Epic, Puranic, and regional mahatmya literature — not a single uniform text — sustain this sense of sacred geography.

Local stories & beliefs

In popular pilgrimage memory, Parshuram Kund is especially associated with Makar Sankranti fair · Northeast Hindu pilgrimage search. Guides and elders retell this identity to first-time visitors as the living ‘reason’ the tirtha draws crowds.

Local sthala-purana and priestly teaching at Parshuram Kund elaborate how Lord Parshuram / sacred kund blesses devotees who arrive with vows, gratitude, or grief. Festival days intensify these beliefs through processions, special alankara, and community feeding where practised.

Pilgrims commonly link practical customs — dress, queue, prasadam, and parikrama — with spiritual fruit. Such customs are living belief: they vary by region and should be followed as posted by the temple trust or endowment board.
