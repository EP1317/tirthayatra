Arunachaleswarar Temple

India · Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu

Agni (fire) Sthalam — the Arunachala hill and temple of Tiruvannamalai.

Salutation to Shiva of Arunachala — the hill of fire

Tamil Shaiva praise of the Agni Sthalam

Significance in mythology

Arunachaleswarar embodies the fire (Agni) element of the Pancha Bhuta. The Arunachala hill itself is worshipped as a lingam of fire — a theology celebrated in Tamil Shaiva literature and later in Ramana Maharshi’s association with the hill.

Pilgrimage literature treats Arunachaleswarar Temple as a tirtha — a crossing where devotion to Lord Shiva (Agni Lingam — Fire) is believed to bear fruit more readily than in ordinary space. Accounts summarised here follow widely cited epic, Puranic, and regional mahatmya traditions; temple priests remain the guides for ritual detail.

Local stories & beliefs

Karthigai Deepam — the beacon on the hill — is the year’s climax of fire theology made visible. Girivalam (circumambulation of Arunachala) is believed to burn karma.

Ashram culture and classical temple ritual coexist in local sacred life.

First-time visitors often hear several versions of the same story from pandas, taxi guides, and elderly pilgrims — a reminder that local belief is living conversation, not a single fixed text.
