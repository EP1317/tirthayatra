Hinglaj Mata Temple

Pakistan · Hinglaj, Lasbela, Pakistan

Hinglaj Mata Temple is counted among the 51 Shakti Peethas. Tradition associates this seat with the Brahmarandhra (crown) of Sati.

Mythology & Significance

Significance in mythology

The Shakti Peetha cycle rests on the Puranic narrative of Daksha’s yajna, Sati’s self-immolation, and Shiva’s grief-stricken tandava. Vishnu’s Sudarshana chakra is said to have dismembered Sati’s body; where each portion fell, a peetha of the Goddess arose.

Hinglaj Mata Temple at Hinglaj, Lasbela, Pakistan is counted among these seats in widely followed pilgrimage lists. Tradition in the popular 51-peetha pilgrimage enumeration associates this seat with the Brahmarandhra (crown) of Goddess Sati. Scriptural enumerations differ (often 51, 52, or 108); Devi / Kalika Purana traditions and later Pithanirnaya-style lists underpin how pilgrims map this geography today.

Goddess Hingula is worshipped here as the living Shakti of the peetha — fierce or gentle according to local form, yet always as the mother who sanctifies place through the Sati–Shiva story.

Local stories & beliefs

At Hinglaj Mata Temple, priests and elders retell the peetha association (Brahmarandhra (crown)) as the reason devotees undertake long journeys for darshan of Goddess Hingula. Navaratri, local jatras, and family kuladevi vows intensify belief that the Goddess of this seat answers specifically here.

Offerings, colours of flowers or cloth, and animal-sacrifice customs (where still practised under temple rules) vary by region — pilgrims are taught to follow only what the local peetha permits.

Because peetha lists themselves vary, local tradition at the shrine is treated as authoritative for ritual detail, while the pan-Indian Sati narrative supplies the shared mythological frame.
