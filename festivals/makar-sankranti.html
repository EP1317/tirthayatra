Makar Sankranti

मकर संक्रांति

Sun enters Capricorn — harvest thanks, kite skies, and sesame–jaggery blessings.

Meaning

मकर संक्रांति मुख्यतः सौर कैलेंडर से जुड़ा पर्व है। सूर्य की उत्तरायण यात्रा और मकर राशि में प्रवेश का दिन — सूर्य आराधना, फसल का आभार और तिल–गुड़ का आदान–प्रदान।

Makar Sankranti is one of the few Hindu festivals tied primarily to the solar calendar. It marks the sun’s northward journey (Uttarayana) and the shift into Makara (Capricorn). Across India it is a harvest thanksgiving — a day to honour Surya, share sweets, and begin a brighter half of the year.

The story

पुराण कथा में संक्रांति सूर्य की उत्तरायण ओर मुड़ने का समय है — महाभारत में भी इसे शुभ काल से जोड़ा जाता है। किसान सूर्य और धरती का आभार मानते हैं; बड़े कहते हैं — तिल–गुड़ की तरह वाणी और संबंध भी मधुर हों।

In Puranic telling, Sankranti is when the sun turns toward the north — a time the Mahabharata associates with auspicious departure and fresh resolve. Farmers thank the sun and earth for grain; elders remind the young that sweetness (jaggery) and warmth (sesame) should season speech and relationships as the cold eases.

Mythological significance

सूर्य को फसल पकाने वाली दिव्य ज्योति माना जाता है। पोंगल, लोहड़ी, माघ बिहू, उत्तरायण — सभी इसी सौर मोड़ की क्षेत्रीय बहनें हैं।

Surya is worshipped as the visible form of divine light that ripens crops and sustains life. Regional sisters of this day — Pongal in Tamil Nadu, Lohri in Punjab, Magh Bihu in Assam, Uttarayan in Gujarat — share the same solar turning while dressing it in local song, food, and fire.

Deity stories

Surya

Devotees offer water (arghya) to the rising sun, remembering him as healer and giver of clarity. Temple towns with Surya associations — and riverbanks for ritual baths — draw pilgrims on this day.

Earth & harvest deities

The festival quietly honours Annapurna and the land that feeds the household — first grains, new rice, and cattle care appear in many regional rites.

How it is celebrated

  • Offer Surya arghya; take a ritual bath where custom allows
  • Exchange til–gud (sesame–jaggery) sweets and greetings
  • Fly kites (especially Gujarat / north India); share harvest meals
  • Donate food, blankets, or grain — daan is central to the day

For the diaspora

विदेश के मंदिर संक्रांति/पोंगल सप्ताहांत पर खिचड़ी, तिल लड्डू और बच्चों के कार्यक्रम रखते हैं। फ्लैट में भी मिठाई बाँटना आसान है।

Temples abroad often hold Sankranti / Pongal weekend programmes with khichdi, til laddoo, and children’s kite crafts. Apartment communities share sweets even when outdoor kite flying is limited.

  • North & West India: Kites, til sweets, river baths; Uttarayan fervour in Gujarat.
  • East India: Magh Bihu / Poush Sankranti feasts and community fires.
  • Diaspora: Temple harvest brunches; combine with Thai Pongal where South Indian.
